Families and the product

01What problem are you solving?

Nothing connects the people around a death. A family deals with the funeral home, the cemetery, the stonemason, the crematorium and the gardener separately, mostly on paper, under rules that change from one town to the next. It sees only part of the options and prices, and only for the few weeks around the funeral. The decades after, from the headstone to grave care, are left to the family to sort out. Each provider works in its own system, so bookings, forms and orders travel by phone, fax and paper. And the people who run these processes are retiring just as deaths rise until about 2050.

02When do families come to mellem?

Before a death, not only after it. People plan ahead with their family, at their own pace and from anywhere, without everyone having to be in one place. When someone dies, they are guided through the first hours and days. And for decades after, they take care of the grave, flowers and remembrance. mellem is there before, around and after the funeral.

03Surely a funeral home already does all that?

No. A funeral home covers a short period around the burial and sells its own range of services and products. On mellem, families see every option and price, choose for themselves and handle what lasts for decades, from the headstone to grave care years later.

04Isn’t that too much for someone who is grieving?

Families can still walk into a funeral home and let it take care of everything. If that funeral home uses mellem, it can share materials and suggestions that the family reviews at home, at their own pace, together with relatives.

05Will families really use it?

They already search online; what is missing is one place where the information and the providers are connected. Families use mellem in two ways: they explore offers and providers directly, or they follow their own guided journey, built around what they need right now. They meet mellem through partners they already trust, and measuring the online share of sales is a core goal of our proof phase.

Partners and competition

06What are you not doing?

We don’t arrange funerals and we don’t build a digital funeral home. We don’t replace the ERP and cemetery software providers already use; we connect to it. We don’t do the work on site: the funeral, the stonework and grave care stay with local providers, who receive the orders through mellem. We don’t charge families, and we don’t sell clicks or advertising: providers get families who have already said what they need. And we don’t spread ourselves thin: Germany first, cluster by cluster, with Finland as our test market.

07Won’t funeral homes see you as a competitor?

No. We don’t arrange funerals and we don’t build a digital funeral home. Funeral homes get qualified leads (families who have already said what they need, where and when), visibility and reach, a place in the journey earlier, before a death, and direct booking of cemetery and crematorium slots.

08Isn’t this a cemetery admin tool?

Mapping and the admin portal are the free or low-cost way in, not the business. They make each cemetery’s graves digital so it can join the network. Revenue comes from what flows through the connections.

09Why won’t ERP vendors block or copy you?

We connect to their systems by email events or API instead of replacing them, so no cemetery has to choose between us and its ERP. An ERP vendor serves one stakeholder group; mellem connects all of them.

10What about Memora?

Memora (Denmark) raised €1.7M in November 2026 and is the closest to us. It digitises the work inside cemeteries, in one country with one burial law. mellem connects everyone around a death, from the family to the cemetery, funeral home, stonemason and crematorium. Germany alone has about 15 times as many cemeteries as Denmark.

11What is your moat?

Connections and data. Every grave has a digital identity, and every task, booking and purchase runs through mellem: a dataset nobody else has, and the base for our own AI. Each new stakeholder makes a cluster more valuable for the next, and every partner brings its own families.

Business model and go-to-market

12How do you make money?

Free for families. Revenue comes from what flows through the network: partner subscriptions from €0 to €99 a month, a 10% standard transaction fee on graves, care orders, headstones and flowers (up to 30% with some partners), and fees for digital forms. The biggest share long term is the aftermarket: one grave earns for decades, from the headstone to yearly care, renewals and remembrance.

13How do you go to market?

Cluster by cluster, through partners. We start with the early adopters in a cluster, bring every local partner on board, and each partner brings its own families. Industry presence and lobbying, local PR and events, and partner organisations make each new cluster easier to win.

14What does one cluster look like?

Take Achim near Bremen: 33,000 people, 3 funeral homes, 5 cemeteries, 1 stonemason, 1 crematorium and 1 cemetery gardener. About 350 deaths a year at an average of €12,000 is €4.2M a year. If a quarter of that runs through mellem at a 10% fee, that is about €100k a year from one cluster, from transaction fees alone. Bringing it live also tells us our CAC per cluster.

15Public cemeteries buy slowly. How do you get in?

Through the early adopters in each cluster, a low entry price and one shared channel for sales, forms and bookings. Our network helps: the Verband der Friedhofsverwalter reaches about 700 cemetery administrators in 11 regional groups.

16Why Germany first?

About 32,000 cemeteries and 16 state burial laws, plus each cemetery’s own statutes. In Germany we already work in 16 “countries”: if it works here, it works anywhere. Finland runs alongside as our test market. Then Austria and Switzerland, then the Netherlands, France and the Nordics, where rules are simpler and partners fewer and bigger.

17Why now?

Peak demand meets the smallest workforce. The boomers are retiring now, so the people who run these processes disappear, while the same generation dies in growing numbers until about 2050. The processes still run on paper. Whoever connects the industry now becomes its infrastructure.

Traction, plan and funding

18Why you?

We have worked in this industry since 2013 and know its people and processes from the inside. We co-lead the Fachgruppe Friedhof at the Bundesverband Bestattungsbedarf and build mellem with strategic partners such as the Verband der Friedhofsverwalter, Letzte Wege neu gedacht, the Trauer Taskforce and, in Finland, Kirkkohallitus.

19Why did you pivot?

Our two paying B2B pilots, before mellem, showed that a stand-alone cemetery tool means fighting entrenched ERP vendors, and that the real gap is that nothing connects the stakeholders. We founded mellem in December 2025 to close that gap, kept what we had built and now sell with partners instead of against them.

20What will you prove in 2027?

In the first 3–4 months: launch, the first cluster live and the first sales, with AI integration from the start. By the end of 2027: 9 clusters and 45 cemeteries, real sales with a measured online share, and a repeatable cluster recipe.

21What is your customer acquisition cost?

We don’t know it yet, and we won’t guess. We measure it per cluster in the proof phase.

22How much are you raising and what for?

€250k pre-seed for a 9-month proof phase, which could be topped up by public funding such as Innovationsstarter. The money goes into AI integration on our own servers, the cluster approach with the first clusters live, and a lean team: the existing team plus freelancers and partners. So far we have bootstrapped, together with IFB Innovationsstarter (InnoFounder).

Data, trust and AI

23Who owns the data?

mellem. Families and partners enter their details on mellem, under our terms of use and partner contracts.

24This is a sensitive industry. What about privacy and security?

Our users are the living, and we protect their data under GDPR. We collect as little sensitive data as possible, grief-support use stays anonymous, and access follows real legal status, such as the grave-rights holder. Data is encrypted, client and server are separated, and everything is hosted in the EU. A GDPR and payments legal review is underway.

25What role does AI play?

Apart from the funeral itself and the work on site, almost every step in between can be supported by AI, for families and for partners. Our own AI, on our own servers, starts learning from launch, while partners pay for the service.
